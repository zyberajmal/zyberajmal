# Public Project Case Study Template

This structure provides the common framework for detailed project pages in this portfolio. It is designed to show engineering thinking, implementation experience and business value while keeping confidential information out of the public repository.

Not every initiative follows the same delivery pattern. Assessment, governance, operational and continuous-improvement projects may adapt individual sections while preserving the same core story: business context, problem, approach, contribution, validation, value and lessons learned.

## 1. Project Overview

Briefly explain the technology area, the purpose of the initiative and the type of environment involved.

## 2. Business Context

Describe why the project mattered to the organization. Focus on the operational requirement, risk, business need or security objective.

## 3. Problem or Requirement

Explain the specific problem that needed to be solved or the requirement that needed to be addressed.

## 4. Existing Challenges

Document the practical constraints that influenced the work. Examples can include legacy systems, user impact, operational dependencies, visibility gaps, support requirements or implementation complexity.

## 5. Research and Evaluation

Summarize how the available options, vendor guidance, security recommendations, technical requirements and operational considerations were evaluated.

## 6. Design Approach

Explain the selected technical approach and the reasoning behind important design decisions.

## 7. Implementation or Review Activity

Describe the work at a useful professional level. Depending on the initiative, this may include deployment phases, configuration areas, assessment activities, stakeholder coordination, operational changes or remediation planning without publishing sensitive implementation details.

## 8. Security and Governance Considerations

Explain how security controls, access, logging, policy, governance, risk and operational responsibilities were considered during the project.

## 9. Validation

Describe how the work was checked. This can include functional testing, security validation, monitoring, stakeholder review, user testing, evidence review or operational verification.

## 10. Business and Operational Value

Explain what improved as a result of the work. Focus on areas such as risk reduction, visibility, consistency, operational efficiency, supportability, user experience, decision quality or governance.

## 11. My Contribution

Make the individual contribution clear. Separate personal responsibility from wider team or organizational activity and avoid implying ownership of work that was only supported or reviewed.

## 12. Documentation and Knowledge Transfer

List the types of documentation, procedures, operational guidance or knowledge transfer created as part of the project.

## 13. Lessons Learned

Capture practical lessons, improvements identified during the project and considerations that would influence future implementations or reviews.

## Public Information Checklist

Before publishing a case study, confirm that it does not contain:

- Internal IP addresses or hostnames
- Credentials, tokens or secrets
- Customer or employee information
- Non-public architecture details
- Sensitive screenshots
- Proprietary configuration exports
- Internal URLs
- Confidential company information
- Security weaknesses that could create unnecessary exposure
- Exact internal control values that do not need to be public
- Claims or metrics that cannot be supported

Where technical context is useful, sensitive details should be generalized while preserving the engineering decisions and lessons learned.
